01About

I am a research assistant working with Prof. Wei Cai on information and coordination in civil aviation. My current work uses event-centred interviews and qualitative analysis to examine how practitioners assess changing operational conditions and coordinate across roles.

I completed a Bachelor of Science at the University of British Columbia, majoring in Computer Science with a minor in Commerce. I am interested in how technical systems, human judgement and organisational practices shape decisions, both in aviation and more broadly.

03Current research

Civil aviation · Qualitative HCI

Information and Coordination in Civil Aviation

Qualitative study · China’s civil aviation system · 23 practitioner interviews

Manuscript submitted · 2026

My contribution

I proposed the research direction and led the work from framing research questions and selecting methods to manuscript preparation. My responsibilities included contacting prospective interview participants, designing the interview questions, reviewing the literature, organising the data and drafting the manuscript.

The study was developed with methodological guidance from Prof. Wei Cai and support from a collaborator on data analysis and manuscript revision.

Research motivation
Reading about the 22 March 2026 runway collision involving Air Canada Express Flight 8646 at New York’s LaGuardia Airport prompted me to think more about the pressures and competing demands that air traffic controllers face. I wanted to explore whether better information and coordination tools could reduce routine manual work and improve overall operational efficiency, giving controllers more capacity to focus on critical judgements and communication. This motivation led me towards studying practitioners’ information needs and cross-role coordination.
Research question
How do air traffic practitioners judge whether changing information warrants an operational response, and what do they check across roles?
Method
The study analyses 23 retrospective interviews using event-centred hybrid coding and Framework Method comparison.
Analytical focus
The analysis distinguishes whether a response remains necessary, whether it can be carried out, and what different forms of cross-role checking establish.
Study scope and limitations

The interviews describe practitioners’ retrospective accounts. They do not independently establish event timing, receiver reasoning, or causal outcomes, and the sample is not representative of the air-traffic workforce.

Implications for computational support are directions for future evaluation. The study does not establish an AI system’s effectiveness or safety.

Emerging directions

These are exploratory directions building on my current work. Their methods and evaluation plans are still being developed.

Human–AI Reassessment
How can people and AI jointly reassess recommendations when operational conditions change?
Timing of AI Assistance
When should AI offer information to support ongoing work without creating unnecessary interruption?
Cross-role Coordination Records
How can people interpret, check and repair coordination records as they move between roles?
Team Recovery from AI Failures
How can teams rebuild a reliable shared understanding when AI-generated information becomes outdated or unavailable?
